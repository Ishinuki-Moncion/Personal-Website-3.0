## Principles
Tier legend: [VERIFIED] cross-checked across ≥3 board refs · [EXTRACTED] read directly off one primary reference · [DATA] measured pixel value · [WORKING] practitioner judgment/extrapolation, no measurement.

1. [VERIFIED] **Render rain in at least two depth planes, not one uniform veil.** A sparse, high-contrast, motion-blurred NEAR plane (a few bold bright streaks) reads over a dense, low-contrast, hazy FAR plane; ground splash/mist anchors the bottom. Cross-checked: anime draws exactly this (bold foreground streaks + fine background wash + splash), the BR rooftop stacks the same layers, and even snow separates sharp-near from soft-far. Never a single flat particle density. (ref: 021-gow-rain, 016-br1982-58, 024-br2049-062; src: https://wall.alphacoders.com/big.php?i=508521)
2. [VERIFIED] **Gate rain visibility on motivated light — rain is bright only where a source backlights it, and vanishes into the dark elsewhere.** Seawall spray reads only inside the headlamp cone; the rain-room shaft only against its backlight; BR streaks only across the lit window/neon. Don't paint rain as a full-frame overlay of even opacity — a rain scene is mostly black with rain lit in pools. (ref: 023-br2049-060, 025-br2049-064; src: https://film-grab.com/2019/05/24/blade-runner-2049/)
3. [VERIFIED] **Streak vs. discrete droplet is a distance/shutter decision — mix both across depth.** Mid/far and fast rain = elongated motion-blur streaks (roughly ≥1:8 aspect); near-plane, backlit, or slow precipitation = discrete round points (snow flakes, the rain-room's backlit drops, macro beads). Using streaks for the falling wash and discrete drops only for the near/slow layer is what sells "real." (ref: 016-br1982-58, 024-br2049-062, 025-br2049-064; src: https://film-grab.com/2019/05/24/blade-runner-2049/)
4. [VERIFIED] **A droplet on glass must refract an inverted, magnified sample of the frame behind it — it is a lens, not a grey blob.** Macro beads each carry a tiny lensed image of the background; on the streaked-window frames every distant light blooms and smears *through* the drops. This is precisely the site's Joi-restrained lens droplets that sample the live frame — keep them sampling, keep the sample inverted + slightly desaturated. (ref: 018-glass-dew-macro, 017-glass-streak-bokeh, 019-glass-nightcity; src: https://unsplash.com/photos/bokeh-photography-of-glass-window-with-water-dew-eUoDRpt5Lpg)
5. [EXTRACTED] **Behind wet glass, distant point lights become large soft-edged colored discs (bokeh), sized by blur — and the sharp droplet field sits on top as the focal plane.** Render background lights as discs (not points), overlay the in-focus droplets, and the eye reads the glass as the subject and the city as depth. (ref: 017-glass-streak-bokeh, 019-glass-nightcity; src: https://unsplash.com/photos/a-rain-streaked-window-with-a-bokeh-effect-Imvn4PWHPcQ)
6. [EXTRACTED] **Model wet glass as three layers: a fogged base, discrete beads, and occasional vertical rivulets that clear a sharper refracting trail.** Rivulets appear where beads merge and run under gravity, cutting a translucent path through the mist that refracts more crisply than its surroundings. Animate beads accreting → merging → running; don't leave a static bead field. (ref: 019-glass-nightcity, 018-glass-dew-macro; src: https://www.pexels.com/photo/rainy-window-with-blurred-city-lights-28993157/)
7. [VERIFIED] **Atmospheric perspective: collapse distant values toward the scene's dominant hue and kill their contrast as rain/haze deepens.** The Vegas dust reduces a whole figure to a low-contrast silhouette in a uniform orange field with no horizon; the seawall's far side fades to teal-black; the BR rooftop hazes to nothing in depth. Far rain should desaturate and haze — never stay crisp to the horizon. (ref: 003-br2049-042, 023-br2049-060, 016-br1982-58; src: https://film-grab.com/2019/05/24/blade-runner-2049/)
8. [VERIFIED] **Wetness is sold by reflection: mirror every emitter as a softened, vertically-smeared streak in the ground directly below it.** Wet rooftops and asphalt double each neon/lamp as a smeared vertical reflection; the seawall's headlamp lays a bright path on the wet deck. Reflections, not a "gloss" shader parameter, are what make a surface read as rain-wet. (ref: 011-br1982-22, 016-br1982-58, 023-br2049-060; src: https://film-grab.com/2010/06/23/blade-runner/)
9. [EXTRACTED] **Steam/mist is the connective tissue — slow, low-contrast volumes lit by the same motivated sources as the rain.** The noodle bar, the wet rooftop, and the seawall spray all carry soft rising volumes that catch only the local light; they unify near and far and soften hard edges between planes. Layer a slow mist pass tinted by the nearest source. (ref: 009-br1982-06, 011-br1982-22; src: https://film-grab.com/2010/06/23/blade-runner/)
10. [EXTRACTED] **Neon in rain blooms into the wet air and tints the local volume its own hue.** The red readout stripes glow *through* the haze; the fluorescent bar and rooftop neon each halo into the humid air and color it. Give saturated sources a soft atmospheric halo and let that color grade the surrounding rain volume — the air itself carries the neon. (ref: 010-br1982-14, 009-br1982-06; src: https://film-grab.com/2010/06/23/blade-runner/)
11. [EXTRACTED] **Localized rainfall needs three parts: an implied source above, discrete backlit droplets in the shaft, and a splash/wet-ring at the impact point.** The Wallace rain-room reads only because the splash-ring and wet halo sit exactly under the shaft; without the impact the droplets read as fake CG. Any leak, shaft, or rain-through-a-hologram must land somewhere visibly. (ref: 025-br2049-064; src: https://film-grab.com/2019/05/24/blade-runner-2049/)
12. [EXTRACTED] **Lightning is a brief high-key event: a near-white core with exponential falloff into the existing blue-black, clouds lit from the strike outward, ground objects flattened to silhouette.** The bolt's channel is the brightest thing in frame and everything darkens away from it; the flash is directional, not an even full-frame brighten. (Diffuse *sheet*-lightning falloff — a hidden in-cloud source glowing the cloud from within — is extrapolated from this bolt's cloud-illumination — [WORKING].) (ref: 020-lightning-nightsky; src: https://www.pexels.com/photo/lightning-on-night-sky-10514728/)
13. [VERIFIED] **Keep the rain frame near-black; spend brightness only on motivated rain, reflections, and a few emitters.** Every strong rain frame here is mostly deep shadow with lit information occupying a small fraction — the same "light is an event, not a fill" law the globe surface found. A rain scene lit evenly loses all its noir depth. (ref: 016-br1982-58, 023-br2049-060, 025-br2049-064; src: https://film-grab.com/2010/06/23/blade-runner/)
14. [EXTRACTED] **You can imply a downpour economically — heavy rain is read from a few bold streaks + a fine wash + strong ground splash + wet-glass reflection, not from rendering every drop.** The Shinkai frame carries convincing torrential rain with a sparse streak set because the splash-mist and fogged glass do the volume work. This is the performance-friendly path for the site: fake density with splash + reflection rather than particle count. (ref: 021-gow-rain; src: https://wall.alphacoders.com/big.php?i=508521)
15. [WORKING] **Synthesis for the daikieOS rain layer.** Near-black base (P13) + motivated-only rain visibility (P2) + a sparse high-contrast NEAR streak plane over a hazy FAR plane with ground splash (P1, P3) + wet-ground reflections of every emitter (P8) + neon-tinted steam volumes (P9, P10) + Joi-restrained glass droplets that refract the live frame (P4–P6) + a splash/wet-ring wherever localized rain lands (P11) + brief directional sheet-lightning high-key events (P12). The site's existing wave-2 depth "particulation" rain, section-tinted gusts, and frame-sampling lens droplets already align with P1/P3/P4; the elevation targets are P2 (gate rain on motivated light instead of a full-frame veil), P8 + P11 (reflections + splash landings), and P12 (sheet-lightning falloff shaped, not a flat flash). (ref: 016-br1982-58, 025-br2049-064)

## Thin areas / open questions
- **No [DATA] measurements.** Streak aspect ratios, droplet-refraction magnification, bokeh-disc diameter vs. blur, and lightning-falloff curves are all qualitative ([VERIFIED]/[EXTRACTED]/[WORKING]); a [DATA] pass would need a pixel picker on a streak/droplet. Consistent with the library's known "weather/hologram timing/measurement" open debt — not invented here.
- **Sheet lightning proper is a gap.** The captured lightning (020) is a branching *bolt*; true diffuse sheet lightning (a hidden in-cloud source glowing the whole cloud) was not captured, so P12's sheet-diffusion clause is [WORKING] extrapolation. The site's "motivated sheet lightning" would benefit from a dedicated cloud-glow reference (a later capture, or a Houdini/Nuke volumetric).
- **Discrete-particle rain leans on stand-ins.** ArtStation Houdini reels (`403` Cloudflare) and Death Stranding *timefall* (JS-gated) were not curl-capturable, so the discrete-particle target is served by BR2049 snow (024), the rain-room droplets (025), and macro beads (018) rather than a purpose-built particle render. A real timefall/Houdini frame (via MCP-browser fallback) would sharpen the "discrete vs streak" numbers.
- **Rain interacting with a hologram is unphotographed here.** The signature BR2049 "rain falling through Joi" shot was not in the sampled frames (the rain material clustered in the finale); the rain-room (025) is the closest proxy for droplets-in-a-lit-volume. Directly relevant to the site's globe holo + rain overlap — worth a targeted recapture from the BR2049 back half if the elevation brief needs it.
- **Temporal behavior inferred from stills.** Gust shear, droplet run-speed, and the lightning flash envelope are motion questions read off single frames; timing belongs to the motion surface, not measured here.
