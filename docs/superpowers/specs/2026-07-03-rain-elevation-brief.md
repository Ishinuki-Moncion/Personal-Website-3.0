## Current state

The wave-2 weather layer is three cooperating systems in `js/background.js`, all disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion` (a frozen rain frame reads as glitch):

- **Depth rain — camera-space particle planes.** `makeDepthRain()` at `background.js:429–468` builds 3 `THREE.Points` planes on full quality (`background.js:438–440`, z = −4/−7, −6/−11, −9/−16) and 2 on LITE (`background.js:436–437`), each with per-drop speed jitter, `sizeAttenuation`, additive blending, a single cyan `0xbfeaff`, and an elongated streak sprite from `makeStreakTexture()` (`background.js:877–893`). Instantiated at `background.js:573`. Per-frame update `updateDepthRain(dt, flash)` at `background.js:1135–1160`: visibility gates on `sceneState.rain` (`vis > 0.02`, `background.js:1137–1138`); a sinusoidal gust + scroll-driven shear set horizontal drift (`wind`, `background.js:1141`, shear fed from scroll velocity at `background.js:1431`); a `beat` term brightens all rain from `haloPulse`/`lockT`/lightning `flash` (`background.js:1142`); section tint `rainTintK` lerps **layer[0] only** cyan→amber in the `projects` section (`background.js:1143–1144`, colors `RAIN_CYAN`/`RAIN_AMBER` at `background.js:1134`); drops fall + advect by wind and recycle to the top (`background.js:1150–1155`); final opacity is the **flat** product `ud.baseOp * vis * beat` (`background.js:1158`).
- **Rain-on-glass lens droplets (Joi).** IIFE `droplets` at `background.js:580–680`, drawing to the 2D `.scene-droplets` canvas (`index.html:35`, `css/site.css:103`), cap LITE 12 / full 24 (`background.js:585`), refraction on non-LITE only (`background.js:586`). `drawDrop()` (`background.js:606–635`) already **samples the live WebGL frame** (`renderer.domElement`, `background.js:612`), clips to the bead (`background.js:616`), **inverts it 180°** via `ctx.rotate(Math.PI)` (`background.js:617`) at `globalAlpha 0.65` (`background.js:618`), magnified `sr = d.r*2.5` (`background.js:614`) — but samples at **full saturation**. Lifecycle `update()` (`background.js:636–678`): spawn rate rides `sceneState.rain` (`background.js:639`); beads condense → swell (`sit`, `background.js:661–667`) → run with wobble (`background.js:668–674`); trails via `destination-out` decay (`background.js:653–655`); a finished drop just `splice`s off-screen (`background.js:674`) — nothing lands. Runs **after** render so the lens reads this frame's buffer (`background.js:1516`).
- **Sheet lightning.** IIFE `lightning` at `background.js:965–981`: a single 64px **radial-gradient sprite** (soft blue-white blob, gradient `background.js:969–972`), `scale 46×30` (`background.js:977`), parked behind the globe at `(0,6,−30)` (`background.js:978`), additive. `updateLightning(dt)` (`background.js:982–1000`): fires every ~30–72 s (`background.js:988`), randomizes only its x (`background.js:989`), runs a decaying multi-flicker envelope `env` (`background.js:994`), sets its own opacity `env*0.24` (`background.js:995`), and **returns `env` as the `flash`** that uniformly lifts every rain drop through `beat` (`background.js:1433` → `background.js:1142`).
- **The "veil" today.** Rain amount per section is a flat scalar in `sectionStories` (`background.js:1217–1242`: home 1.0, about 0.85, work 0.7, gallery 0.35, projects 0.55, contact 0.8), eased into `sceneState.rain` at `background.js:1424`. That one 0–1 number is the **only** rain-visibility control, and it multiplies opacity uniformly across the whole frame (`background.js:1158`) — a full-frame veil dialed by section, with no spatial idea of *where light is*.

## Target intent

- **Gate rain visibility on motivated light — the biggest elevation vs today's fuller veil.** A rain frame should be mostly black with rain lit only in pools where a lamp/neon/window/strike backlights it, not a full-frame overlay of even opacity. (ref: rain/023)
- **Keep the frame near-black; spend brightness only on motivated rain, reflections, and a few emitters** — the Spine's "light is an event" law, holding on this surface. (ref: rain/016)
- **Hold ≥2 depth planes — sparse high-contrast NEAR streaks over a dense hazy FAR wash**, never one uniform density. (ref: rain/021)
- **Let the far plane obey atmospheric perspective** — distant rain desaturates and hazes toward the scene's dominant hue, never staying crisp cyan to the horizon. (ref: rain/003)
- **Streak vs discrete droplet is a distance/shutter call** — elongated streaks for the fast/far wash, discrete round drops for the near/slow/backlit plane; mix both across depth. (ref: rain/016)
- **Keep the Joi lens droplets sampling the live frame, inverted — and add the missing desaturation** so each bead reads as a slightly-desaturated inverted lens, not a saturated mirror. (ref: rain/018)
- **Render distant point-lights behind wet glass as soft colored bokeh discs** with the sharp droplet field on top as the focal plane. (ref: rain/017)
- **Sell wetness by reflection — mirror every emitter as a soft vertically-smeared streak on the ground below it.** Reflections, not a gloss shader, make a surface read wet. (ref: rain/011)
- **Land localized rainfall in three parts — implied source, backlit droplets in the shaft, and a splash/wet-ring at impact.** Without the landing it reads fake. (ref: rain/025)
- **Make lightning a brief directional high-key event** — near-white core with exponential falloff into the blue-black, the local rain/haze lit from the strike outward, not a flat full-frame brighten. (ref: rain/020)
- **Layer neon-tinted steam/mist as connective tissue** — slow low-contrast volumes catching only the nearest motivated source, tinting the local rain their hue. (ref: rain/010)

## Concrete code levers

- **Lever A — Motivated-light gate `(new)`** (ref: rain/023; rain/016). Today the flat `vis * beat` multiply at `background.js:1158` (and the droplet spawn gate at `background.js:639`) treat rain as spatially uniform. Introduce a screen-space `motivation(pos)` weight and change the terminal opacity to `ud.baseOp * vis * beat * motivation`, so a drop brightens near real emitters and falls toward ~0 in the dark field. The emitter set already exists to sample: the Tokyo-halo glow sprite (`background.js:497–499`), place-node emitters (`background.js:1476–1482`), the callout (`background.js:1472–1473`), the orbital scan ring, and the live lightning x (`background.js:989`). Cheapest first cut: a few additive falloff wells at those projected screen positions, evaluated per plane (not per drop) to bound cost. `(new — no spatial gate today.)`
- **Lever B — Wet-ground emitter reflections `(new)`** (ref: rain/011). No reflection pass exists anywhere. Add a bottom-of-frame "wet ground" band that, for each screen-space emitter (Tokyo-halo glow `background.js:497–499`, place nodes `background.js:1476–1482`, callout `background.js:1472–1473`), draws a **vertically-flipped, vertically-smeared, dimmed** additive copy. Cheapest home is a new draw pass on the existing `.scene-droplets` 2D canvas (the `droplets` IIFE `background.js:580–680`), which already composites over the WebGL frame after render (`background.js:1516`); a dedicated additive plane is the alternative. `(new.)`
- **Lever C — Splash / wet-ring landings `(new)`** (ref: rain/025). Near-plane drops recycle silently to the top (`background.js:1152–1155`) and glass beads just `splice` off-screen (`background.js:674`) — nothing lands. When a near-plane drop crosses the wet-ground band (Lever B) or a motivated shaft, spawn a brief expanding wet-ring + splash-mist sprite that grows and fades over ~0.3 s; keep a persistent wet-ring beneath any localized shaft (e.g. under the Tokyo halo). This is also the performance-friendly density trick — fake a downpour from splash + reflection, not particle count. `(new.)`
- **Lever D — Lens droplet: keep sampling + invert, add desaturation** (ref: rain/018; rain/017). The bead already samples `renderer.domElement` (`background.js:612`), clips (`background.js:616`), and inverts 180° (`background.js:617`) — it just samples at full saturation. In `drawDrop` (`background.js:611–620`), route the sampled region through a desaturate step before compositing (e.g. `ctx.filter = 'saturate(0.5) brightness(0.9)'` into the clipped bead, or a manual chroma-reduce mix) so the lens reads slightly desaturated and inverted. Optional companion: render the out-of-focus city lights behind the sharp bead field as soft bokeh discs (ref: rain/017). *Elevation of an existing mechanism — not new.*
- **Lever E — Preserve ≥2 planes; push far-plane atmospheric haze** (ref: rain/021; rain/003). The plane defs already encode sparse-near / dense-far (`background.js:438–440` full, `background.js:436–437` LITE) — keep them. Elevate the color/opacity handling in `updateDepthRain` (`background.js:1144–1145`): today every layer shares one crisp cyan and only layer[0] tints amber (`background.js:1144`). Desaturate and haze the far/dense layers toward the section base hue with reduced contrast, keeping the near plane sparse and high-contrast — mostly a color/opacity tuning of the existing loop, not a rebuild.
- **Lever F — Directional lightning falloff `(new)`** (ref: rain/020). Two edits. (1) Shape the sprite gradient at `background.js:969–972` from its roughly-linear falloff to **exponential** from the core, so the flash is a near-white centre dissolving into blue-black rather than an even blob. (2) Make the rain flare **directional**: replace the uniform `(flash || 0) * 1.3` inside `beat` (`background.js:1142`) with a per-plane weight that scales the strike's contribution by screen-space distance to `lightning.sp.position.x` (set at `background.js:989`), so drops near the strike over-brighten and far drops barely react. The sprite, decaying envelope (`background.js:994`), and rain-coupling (`background.js:1433`) already exist; the directional weighting is `(new)`.
- **Lever G — Neon-tinted steam/mist connective tissue `(new)`** (ref: rain/010; rain/009). No mist volume exists. Add a slow low-contrast additive haze pass, tinted by the nearest motivated source and lit only where Lever A says light is — it unifies the near/far planes and softens edges. Lowest priority; land Levers A/B/C first. `(new.)`

## Before / after

Today the viewer sees an even cyan rain veil whose density steps down by section, with lens beads mirroring the globe and a soft blue flash that dims the whole rain field at once. After: the frame goes near-black and rain **appears only in lit pools** — under the Tokyo halo, along the scan ring, inside a lightning's reach — every emitter throws a smeared wet reflection onto a glistening ground, drops **land** in splash-rings instead of vanishing, and a strike lights the rain nearest it hardest while the far field stays dark. The beads still refract the live globe, now slightly desaturated and clearly inverted, reading as worn glass rather than a clean mirror.

## Out of scope / risks

- **Performance.** Per-drop distance math on 300+ points and a second reflection/mist canvas pass are the cost risks. Bound Lever A/F to per-plane (not per-drop) weights; cap Lever B/G work; verify on LITE and coarse-pointer devices before shipping. Reflection/splash should degrade first on LITE.
- **Reduced motion.** The whole layer is `null` under `prefers-reduced-motion` (`background.js:573`, `background.js:581`, `background.js:966`). Every lever must no-op there — do not resurrect rain for reduced-motion users.
- **Don't lift the darks.** Gating rain on light must not become a reason to raise the base; the field between pools stays near-black (Spine §0.1; ref: rain/016). One temperature per section holds — the amber `projects` tint (`background.js:1143–1144`) is the only warm exception and should stay a single-section switch, not an in-frame dapple.
- **Known reference gaps (from the study thin-areas), carried as honest debt, not blockers.** True *sheet* lightning (a hidden in-cloud glow) was never captured — rain/020's sheet-diffusion clause is `[WORKING]` extrapolation from a branching bolt, so Lever F's cloud-from-within behavior is unvalidated. Rain falling *through* a hologram (globe holo + rain overlap) is unphotographed — the rain-room (rain/025) is the closest proxy. Discrete-particle rain leans on filmic stand-ins (no Houdini/timefall frame). No `[DATA]` measurements exist for streak aspect, refraction magnification, bokeh-disc size, or falloff curves — all tuning values here are qualitative and should be dialed by eye against the board, not treated as spec.
