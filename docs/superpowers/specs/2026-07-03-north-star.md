# daikieOS — North Star (2026-07-03)

**The single feel the whole site renders toward:**

> A **living instrument booting up in a near-black cyber-noir night** — a quiet, mostly-dark field where a real Earth turns as a cool cyan hologram, city-lights flicker as scarce warm signals, rain lights only where something backlights it, and the interface reads the world through worn projected glass. It should feel *observed and instrumented*, not decorated — like sitting at a calm console that comes alive only where you look.

**Pinned key-art:** `refs/globe/004-nasa-rotate-globe.png` — NASA Black Marble as a rotating sphere: a near-black Earth, a thin cool atmospheric limb, warm city-emitters clustered on the coastlines, everything else deep shadow. That single frame *is* the site's centre of gravity: the globe is the monument, the darkness is the medium, the scarce warm light is the signal. (ref: globe/004 · [EXTRACTED])

## What "rendering toward it" means, surface by surface
The globe is a rimless additive cyan hologram over a real Earth-at-night; rain is gated to motivated light with wet reflections and splash landings; the HUD is one cyan ink with a single amber accent, framed in corner brackets on a faint graticule; motion animates only the focus and acknowledges state with one boxed word; type holds a tracked-mono instrument voice apart from a sentence-case reading voice; color sits crushed at a teal-black value floor with a 1–6% warm-accent budget; composition is negative-space-first with the globe dead-centre as the one monument. All of it is one graded world — see `2026-07-03-design-language-v3.md` for the enforceable rules.

## The four non-negotiables (if only these survive, the feel survives)
1. **Near-black, light-is-an-event.** ~88% of every view sits in deep shadow; never lift the darks to a comfortable grey UI. (ref: color/002 · [DATA])
2. **Cool instrument, warm signal — one temperature per view, warm budgeted to a few percent.** Cyan reads; amber means *something is happening*. (ref: color/004 · [DATA])
3. **Additive and rimless.** Light blooms from a core into a same-hue halo; holograms dissolve into scatter — no hard rims, no flat colored blocks. (ref: globe/006 · [VERIFIED])
4. **Instrument only the focus.** Label, animate, and light the one thing that matters; leave the rest a quiet, faintly-ticked void. (ref: hud/010 · [VERIFIED])

## You'll know it's working when…
…a first-time visitor's eye lands on exactly one thing per screen; the page feels *dark and alive* rather than dark and empty; the amber reads as an alert because it's rare; and the whole thing feels like one continuous graded night, not a stack of styled sections.

## …and failing when
…the darks have crept up to slate-grey; cyan and amber are fighting for equal area; a hologram or card has a crisp hard edge; or three things are animating at once and nothing feels like the focus.
